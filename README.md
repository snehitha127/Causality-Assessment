### ASSESSMENT OF ADVERSE DRUG REACTIONS USING STANDARD CAUSALITY ASSESSMENT METHODS

### 1\. Abstract

Pharmacovigilance plays an important role in identifying, evaluating and preventing adverse drug reactions (ADRs) associated with medicinal products. Adverse drug reactions are harmful and unintended responses that occur following the administration of medicines.

Causality assessment is the systematic evaluation of the relationship between a suspected drug and an observed adverse reaction. It helps determine whether a particular medicine is responsible for the reported reaction.

This project focuses on evaluating suspected adverse drug reactions using two standard methods: the Naranjo Algorithm and the WHO–UMC Causality Assessment System. The assessment includes temporal relationships, dechallenge, rechallenge, alternative causes and clinical evidence. The reactions are classified according to their probability of association with the suspected drug.

### 2\. Introduction

Medicines are essential for the prevention and treatment of various diseases. However, their administration may sometimes result in unwanted and harmful effects known as adverse drug reactions.

ADRs can affect different organs and systems, including the gastrointestinal system, cardiovascular system, nervous system, skin and respiratory system.

The occurrence of adverse drug reactions may depend on several factors, including:

* Drug dosage and duration of treatment.

* Age and individual patient characteristics.

* Genetic factors.

* Drug–drug interactions.

* Drug allergies.

* Renal and hepatic function.

* Existing medical conditions.

Causality assessment is an important process in pharmacovigilance that evaluates the possible relationship between a suspected medicine and an adverse reaction.

### 7\. Naranjo Algorithm

The Naranjo Algorithm is a standardised questionnaire used to determine the probability that an adverse reaction is associated with a suspected drug.

It consists of ten questions:

* Are there previous conclusive reports of this adverse reaction?

* Did the adverse reaction occur after administration of the suspected drug?

* Did the reaction improve after discontinuation of the drug?

* Did the reaction reappear after re-administration of the drug?

* Are there alternative causes that could have produced the reaction?

* Did the reaction reappear after placebo administration?

* Was the drug detected in blood or other body fluids at toxic concentrations?

* Did the reaction become more severe with an increased dose or less severe with a decreased dose?

* Did the patient experience a similar reaction during previous exposure to the same or a similar drug?

* Was the adverse reaction confirmed by objective clinical evidence?

### Naranjo Score Interpretation

| Score      | Classification |
| ---------- | -------------- |
| 9 or above | Definite       |
| 5–8        | Probable       |
| 1–4        | Possible       |
| 0 or below | Doubtful       |

The total score is calculated by adding the individual scores assigned to the ten questions.

### 8\. WHO–UMC Causality Assessment System

The WHO–UMC system is used to assess the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse reaction based on clinical judgement.

### Categories of causality assessment

1\. Certain

The adverse reaction has a plausible time relationship with drug administration. Other diseases or medicines are unlikely to explain the reaction, and clinical evidence strongly supports the relationship.

2\. Probable/Likely

The reaction has a reasonable time relationship with drug administration. Other causes are unlikely, and improvement after discontinuation is clinically reasonable.

3\. Possible

The reaction has a reasonable time relationship with the suspected drug, but other diseases or medicines may also explain the occurrence.

4\. Unlikely

The time relationship makes a causal relationship improbable, and another explanation is more likely.

5\. Conditional/Unclassified

Additional information is required for proper causality assessment.

6\. Unassessable/Unclassifiable

The available information is insufficient or contradictory and cannot be verified or supplemented.

### 9\. Assessment Criteria

### A. Temporal Relationship

Evaluation of whether the adverse reaction occurred after administration of the suspected medicine.

### B. Dechallenge

Assessment of whether the adverse reaction improves after discontinuation of the suspected drug.

### C. Rechallenge

Evaluation of whether the adverse reaction reappears following re-administration of the suspected medicine. Rechallenge should not be performed solely for assessment when it could expose the patient to harm.

### D. Alternative Causes

Evaluation of other possible reasons for the adverse reaction, including underlying diseases, concomitant medications and other clinical conditions.

### E. Previous Documentation

Assessment of whether the suspected adverse reaction has been previously reported or documented in medical literature.

### F. Clinical Outcome

Evaluation of the patient's condition following discontinuation, continuation or modification of the suspected medication.

### 10\. Clinical Case Assessment

Patient Details

* Age: 45 years

* Gender: Female

* Suspected drug: Amoxicillin

* Adverse drug reaction: Skin rash

* Onset: Three days after starting treatment

* Outcome: Recovery after discontinuation of the suspected drug.

### Clinical Assessment

The patient developed a skin rash three days after initiating amoxicillin treatment. The appearance of the reaction after drug administration indicates a temporal relationship.

Following discontinuation of amoxicillin, the patient experienced improvement in the skin rash. This dechallenge response supports a possible association between the suspected drug and the adverse reaction.

However, other possible causes, including underlying infection, concomitant medications and previous allergic reactions, must also be evaluated.

The final causality classification should be determined after reviewing the complete clinical information.

### 13\. Results and Discussion

Causality assessment helps evaluate the possible relationship between suspected medicines and adverse drug reactions.

The Naranjo Algorithm provides a numerical scoring system to estimate the probability of drug-related adverse reactions. The WHO–UMC system provides a structured clinical classification based on available patient information and clinical evidence.

The assessment of temporal relationships, dechallenge, rechallenge and alternative causes plays an important role in determining the likelihood of a causal association.

Both assessment methods contribute to systematic ADR evaluation and support pharmacovigilance activities.

### 14\. Limitations

* Causality assessment depends on the completeness of patient information.

* Some adverse reactions may have multiple possible causes.

* Rechallenge information may not be available.

* Clinical manifestations may vary between patients.

* The assessment methods may not establish definite causation in every case.

* Clinical judgement is essential for accurate classification.
